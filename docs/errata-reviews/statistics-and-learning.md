# Errata in statistical tests, identification, and causal discovery

These reviews compare the original proceedings or named report with the dated correction linked on the author's homepage. The correction PDFs describe their AI-assisted edits as proposed and author review as pending. Lean covers the individual statements named below. The remaining proofs, code behavior, and historical experiment claims have their own evidence requirements.

## Hwang et al. (CLeaR 2023), Neural Contextual Decomposition

Compare the CLeaR paper with the October 25, 2026 correction. Its change summary is on p. 27. The corrected PDF SHA-256 is `1f22d86e47ad3f1013d30237cef37592749538c41e0045caacc75892ae572ad9`. [Proceedings](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v213/hwang23a.html) · [Correction](https://www.sanghacklee.me/ai-corrected/NCD_AI_corrected_2026-10-25.pdf)

| Location | Comparison and verification scope |
| --- | --- |
| Def. 3, Prop. 6, Thm. 7 | The original definition equates conditional densities at every assignment in a context, and Prop. 6 uses convexity. The correction requires a positive-probability context and equality of conditional laws almost surely, with the convex intersection argument on the interior. A zero-weight discrete assignment with a different conditional law shows why the two formulations can differ. The Lean example proves that distinction. The continuous result additionally uses the paper’s strictly positive density setting and the geometry and measure arguments of Apps. B–C. |
| App. B, Props. 16–17 | The original coordinate-wise connectivity criterion applies to general contexts. The revised claims require an open representative of positive probability and test the fiber connectivity of that representative. A continuous path through a null-width connector can link regions while imposing no almost-sure equality of kernels there. The local product-neighborhood and positive-measure overlap steps explain why the topology of the representative matters. A general Lean treatment of those steps remains open. |
| §§3–4, Def. 9, Prop. 12 | The original moves from conditional independences to regular contextual decomposition with unconditional regularity language. The revision separates the cellwise equivalence for each positive-probability partition cell from regularity, maximality and residual-cell requirements. A sufficient set can still have a redundant input, so the cellwise equivalence on its own cannot justify a regular parent set. The residual-cell and uniqueness arguments remain open in Lean. |
| §5.1, App. E.1 | The original main text and appendix use different gate-input index descriptions. The correction keeps the descriptions identifiable and records the missing historical generator. The actual index map and its effect on the reported ROC curves remain unresolved. |
| App. A, Prop. 14, and local repairs | The original proof switches the roles of the fixed and varying coordinates. The corrected discrete argument uses a fixed-coordinate fiber for CSI and intersects that fiber with the PCI domain for PCI. Both contexts must have positive probability. Lean proves the two implications by keeping the CSI conditional law or taking the common supported PCI law. In the latter case every supported assignment has that law, so averaging would give the same witness. The local proof and set-difference edits leave general neural consistency and optimality open. |
| Eqs. (4)–(5), implementation and experiments | The correction records that the currently linked continuous multi-mask Gaussian aggregation differs from the stated density mixture. That released implementation is shared with FGCDL. Historical configurations and rerun evidence would be needed to attribute the difference to the CLeaR curves. The numerical values and figures are retained in the correction. |

The formal statements are `csi_implies_cssi`, `pci_implies_cssi`, `cssi_congr_on_support` and `zero_mass_changed_law`. On finite discrete probability spaces, existence of a supported assignment in the context expresses positive probability. The abstract output type allows equality of entire conditional laws. ## Lee and Bareinboim (NeurIPS 2021), Causal Identification with Matrix Equations

The October 25, 2026 correction names the August 2022 technical report R-70 as its comparison copy. Its change summary is on p. 23. The corrected PDF SHA-256 is `a191bd0124d2087d74dbdcc3a91e9dc587a82ceb19b4b9fa3103ec8895878946`. [Proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2021/hash/4ea06fbc83cdd0a06020c35d50e1e89a-Abstract.html) · [Technical report R-70](https://causalai.net/r70.pdf) · [Correction](https://www.sanghacklee.me/ai-corrected/matrix_equations_AI_corrected_2026-10-25.pdf)

| Location | Comparison and verification scope |
| --- | --- |
| Thm. 1, Eq. (1), App. C | R-70 gives full column rank but uses ordinary-inverse notation in reconstruction, and Thm. 1 leaves overlap implicit. The revision keeps the common latent column domain, uses a Moore–Penrose left inverse at the stated rank, and requires positive observed weight wherever the target latent weight is positive. Only those states enter elementwise division. Lean proves the weighted reconstruction in case (2a) from an explicit left inverse and three proxy factorizations. In case (2b), it applies the inverse for each outcome before summing to recover observed latent weights, and uses the selected outcome inverse on the surrogate proxy. The three-by-two algebraic example gives `1/4` under its supplied kernel slices. Summing the observed proxy before applying the selected inverse instead gives `1`. Deriving the factorizations from the graphical assumptions and the pseudoinverse identity from full column rank remain open. |
| Thm. 2, condition (3), App. C | R-70 states independence of the proxy from the complement of the selected variables given the selected variables and latent variables. The correction uses `W ⟂ S ∣ U,S′`, where `S′ = (X ∪ Z) ∖ S`. When `Z′` is nonempty every reconstruction slice at fixed `z′` must be invertible. The proof uses a varying slice in reconstruction and the selected slice with the surrogate distribution. The change reverses the roles of the variable sets, and applying a single available inverse throughout would skip the new slice condition. The generalized criterion is outside the formal result. |
| Thm. 3, App. D, Figs. 11–12 | R-70 imposes `Z ∩ Xk = ∅` and says the nesting affects the restriction on `Z`. The correction also imposes `W ∩ Xi = ∅` for the displayed ordering. It uses `W = {11}`, fixes variable 12 consistently, and changes the highlighted diagram region. The intermediary identity with repeated assignments and the figure computation remain open in Lean. |
| Alg. 1, Thms. 4–5 | Line 4 now projects only onto subsets of the outcome ancestors. The graph used for proxy checks still permits non-ancestor proxies. PROXY gains the rectangular rank form in Eq. (1). The revised proofs state a composition invariant and argue subsumption of the original proxy criteria, GID(-PO), mID and eID in the discrete setting. The chain example supplies strictness. These require verification of the whole ID-ME composition separately from the pointwise left-inverse result. |
| §4.1, appendices and figures | The corrected Fig. 3b criterion uses `P(W∣U,x,Y)` and `Pₓ,ᵧ(W)`. The two-factor proof keeps the common assigned treatment values. Other repairs include the adjustment criterion's `W ∉ X`, do-calculus graph notation, the Thm. 3 set difference, slice notation, latent-DAG separation and operand labels. These checks compare the cited expressions across editions. The graph and factorization lemmas themselves remain outside the Lean module. |

Case (2a) uses `recover_adjustment_term` and verifies one rectangular inverse explicitly. Case (2b) uses `recover_observed_marginal` and `outcome_indexed_identification`. The latter assumes the observed and interventional proxy factorizations and the chain and adjustment identities on target support. When a latent state has zero target weight, its contribution is zero without dividing by its observed weight. ## Lee and Honavar (UAI 2017), Self-Discrepancy Conditional Independence Test

The September 19, 2026 correction names the camera-ready source including Apps. A–B as its base and appends the expanded proof in App. C. The proceedings PDF and the author-hosted edited predecessor supply the comparison here. The change summary is on p. 14. The corrected PDF SHA-256 is `4ad97e40059c1b10bd0e3c3963c192970292632b11e26d8ea5a25f0e626aaff9`. [Proceedings PDF](https://www.auai.org/uai2017/proceedings/papers/16.pdf) · [Edited predecessor](https://www.sanghacklee.me/assets/SDCIT-edited.pdf) · [Correction](https://www.sanghacklee.me/ai-corrected/SDCIT_AI_corrected_2026-09-19.pdf)

| Location | Comparison and verification scope |
| --- | --- |
| Abstract, §4.1, Eq. (2) | The original invokes independence of retained observations and an unbiased MMD estimate. Excluding terms with overlapping indices reduces direct reuse. A learned permutation can still depend on other observations, so exclusion supplies neither the stated independence nor finite-sample unbiasedness. The revised statement keeps residual dependence and treats convergence separately. |
| Thm. 1, App. C | The original appeals to the earlier convergence argument and uses `8C²/(n−1)` for deletion. The revision assumes i.i.d. observations on compact metric spaces, fixed bounded continuous kernels with universal product, a Z-only derangement with independent auxiliary randomness, and average RKHS distortion going to zero in probability. It removes a conditional-mean continuity requirement. Lean proves the denominator identity and the `16C²/(n−1)` scalar bound over rationals given the omitted-count and sum bounds. A signed bounded scalar instance rules out inferring the smaller constant solely from those bounds. Whether such terms are realizable by the paper's feature differences is a separate question. The conditional-dependence argument, feature law of large numbers, and convergence of the raw statistic remain outside Lean. Test level and consistency remain separate from raw-statistic convergence. |
| §4.2, App. B | The original pair-count argument motivates halving the scale of subsample fluctuations. The revision treats this rescaling and the regression adjustment empirically and specifies the implemented adjustment. The comparison has no derivation of general calibration or justification for adaptive distances and bandwidths. |
| §4.3, Alg. 4 | The original pseudocode applies an infinite penalty to `I`, although that set contains retained pairs. The revision applies `Dmax` to the excluded off-diagonal pairs `Jπ`. The finite penalty can be zero for a zero distance matrix. Feasibility and exclusion cannot be inferred from the soft penalty, which also leaves historical reference samples unresolved. |
| §4.4 | The original Algorithm 4 leaves the tail convention implicit. The historical implementation uses the strict upper tail, which gives zero when the observed and centered reference statistics tie at zero. The correction and current public implementation count ties and add one to both the numerator and denominator. Lean computes zero for the strict score and one for the corrected score at constant data, for every reference length, with Lean's total division convention at length zero. The empirical procedure presumes a positive reference count. A valid reference distribution and general test calibration remain open. |
| §§3.1, 5.2–5.4 | The correction qualifies the statements on monotone power, equal power and kernel robustness, and fixes the Blossom V attribution. Equivalent formulations of the CI null give no basis for different target nulls. Matching reference variance at `B = 2145` uses null data and cannot establish power under alternatives. Increasing `B` can reduce simulation variability within a dataset while between-dataset variability persists. |
| §5.1, Figs. 1–6 | The correction changes the squared term's sign in the coupled Hénon equation and separates independent noises in the post-nonlinear null generator. The printed and Python Hénon lag coefficient for the Y equation is `0.3`. Archived MATLAB uses `0.1` for that coefficient. The correction leaves the coefficient used for plotted data unresolved. The serial Hénon observations fall outside the i.i.d. convergence theorem. |
| Fig. 2 | The original bottom-row caption says CHSIC is excluded. The revised caption reports its inclusion for dimensions 1–5, with AUPC for dimensions 3–5 below the displayed range. Plot values and limits are retained. |
| Throughout, code and experiments | The revision keeps venue, year, authorship, numerical results and figures, with notation, English and citation edits. It records targeted defects in native matrix shuffling, matching-cost arithmetic and strict-tail handling. The effect of those changes on the historical comparisons remains unmeasured. |

The formal statements include `renormalized_difference`, `renormalized_deletion_bound`, `mmsd_deletion_bound`, `original_constant_fails_for_bounded_terms` and `constant_data_tail`. ## Hwang et al. (ICML 2024), Fine-Grained Causal Dynamics Learning

Compare the ICML paper with the October 25, 2026 correction. Its change summary is on p. 31. The corrected PDF SHA-256 is `c8ca9632089bd292ceb20391743a61c0cd0b659477bd047e0bf18afb71fe927b`. [Proceedings](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/hwang24b.html) · [Correction](https://www.sanghacklee.me/ai-corrected/FGCDL_AI_corrected_2026-10-25.pdf)

| Location | Comparison and verification scope |
| --- | --- |
| Defs. 3–6, Assumption 2, Thm. 1, Lemmas 4–5 | The original pointwise definition takes conditioning sets only among global parents. The corrected law is almost sure on positive-probability cells and permits any current state or action variables in a sufficient set. Local faithfulness means every such set contains the local parents. Lean proves restriction of sufficiency and the inclusion of the smaller context's parents under this faithfulness assumption, using discrete support. The continuous implication uses absolute continuity upon restriction. Null cells impose no constraint on graph identification. The full score and density characterization in Thm. 1 remain open in Lean. |
| §3.4, Def. 7, Assumption 5, Prop. 1 | The corrected maximization is over a specified class `T` with faithful positive-probability cells and a uniform positive bound on the relevant loss separation `η`. The positive lower bound allows the same sparsity penalty throughout `T`. The revised Gaussian example with shrinking cells shows how the infimum can vanish even when each individual cell has a positive separation. Optimization or consistency of the trained quantized system remains separate from this population proposition. |
| Thm. 2, Def. 9 | The original asserts availability of a comparison decomposition from `K ≥ H`. The corrected theorem additionally requires a refinement of the canonical partition in `T`. Each canonical context must preserve its LCG on every positive-probability subset. Lean proves any positive atom supplies a singleton with an empty sufficient input set. Faithfulness there forces the canonical parent set to be empty. Consequently the theorem cannot identify discrete Chemical contexts with nonempty LCGs. The general refinement theorem remains open in Lean. |
| §§4.1–4.2, Figs. 5–9 and 11, App. C | The corrected descriptions identify the plotted graphs as experimental references. A reference graph can keep a parent whose value is fixed by the context. Lean proves dropping such a coordinate preserves sufficiency, which makes that edge incompatible with inclusion-minimality there. SHD against definition-based LCGs and the associated recovery comparisons remain unevaluated. |
| §3.3, Apps. C.1 and C.4 | The corrected text flags sequential state updates that can violate next-state factorization and distinguishes the decoder and EMA updates from the population objective. The shared release computes one Gaussian from averaged means and aggregated scales in its continuous multi-mask branch. The Chemical default takes the categorical single-mask path. The actual historical settings and their applicability to each plotted run remain unresolved. |
| App. A.3, §5 | The correction attributes `β/α > 3` to a fitted smoking and blood-pressure model in Nyman et al. The example does not establish an intervention effect. |
| Proofs, §§3–4, Apps. C–D, references and captions | The correction restores the missing `λ` in a formula and adjusts notation, loss and gradient descriptions, finite graph-output wording, attribution and comparisons. The revision records that `K = 2` does not rank worst in every entry of Table 3 and that NPS is an exception to the training comparison in Table 1. The figures and numerical results are retained, and reproducibility of the benchmark remains open. |

The formal statements are `sufficient_of_subcontext`, `local_parents_of_subcontext`, `remove_fixed_input` and `canonical_context_with_positive_atom_has_no_parents`. They treat one next-state coordinate at a time.
