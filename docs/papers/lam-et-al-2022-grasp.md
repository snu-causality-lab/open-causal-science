## Tuck and covered reversal

`OrderSplit.tuck` implements Definition 4.1 on the three blocks around the ordered endpoints. It uses the supplied DAG's ancestor predicate to move the middle ancestors of the head ahead of both endpoints. The other middle vertices follow both endpoints. Each block keeps its relative order. `OrderSplit.tuck_perm` proves the result is a permutation of the input, and `OrderSplit.from_order_original` and `OrderSplit.reconstructs_order` connect the executable split to its list decomposition.

`covered_singular` and `singular_all` prove the inclusions between the three edge tiers in Definition 4.2. For a covered edge in a DAG with the input order as a topological order, `covered_tuck_order` proves that the middle ancestor block is empty. `covered_tuck_is_causal_order` then proves Lemma B.8(a) using the existing Chickering covered reversal on the supplied DAG. The supplement proves the bound using the intermediate covered reversal $H$. The last equality in the proof compares $|E(H)|$ with $|E(G_\tau)|$. ## P-minimality and consistency

The ordered graph is Markov and edge-deletion minimal under a matching graphoid relation, the (RU) instance of Theorem 3.5. The adjacent-transposition lemmas in the supplement are also formalized. Theorem 4.5 uses the formalized inclusion transformation of Chickering (2002). It converts covered reversals and deletions into a sequence of covered tucks while maintaining subgraph containment with respect to the intermediate DAGs. A non-P-minimal order therefore has a covered improvement. Repeated improvements reach a P-minimal order, and a witness to the next improvement needs at most `n!` moves.
