## Order search in Algorithms 4–5

`mem_insertions_iff` characterizes every relocation of one vertex as a cut of the fixed list of other vertices. `algorithm_five_eq_best_move` proves that the successive position trials and rollback in Algorithm 5 agree with an incumbent-preserving maximum over those relocations for a duplicate-free order. Both versions accept only strict score increases. `sweep_fixed_at_equal_score` proves that a full sweep with no score gain leaves the order unchanged and rules out improvement by every one-vertex move. `search_perm_and_locally_optimal` proves that repeated full sweeps terminate at such an order and preserve the vertex permutation, for an arbitrary fixed rational order score. ## Grow–shrink and projection in Sections 2–3

`ranked_improvements_filter` proves that filtering a cached ranking by the eligible prefix gives the same ranking as scoring and sorting only eligible candidates, with label-based tie breaking. `tree_query_eq_grow_shrink` proves that a finite tree caching ranked additions, shrunken parents and scores gives the same parents and score as running grow–shrink again for that prefix. `shrink_properties` proves that each returned parent set is a subset of the input, has at least its input score, and admits no improving deletion. `project` uses the predecessor check to construct a ranked DAG. The reference tree is built eagerly. The score-gain guards make explicit when Algorithms 1–2 stop, as in the authors' implementation. [Lean](../../OpenCausalScience/Papers/AndrewsEtAl2023/GrowShrinkTree.lean) · [Public implementation](https://github.com/bja43/boss/blob/bf937196adac74b7be157b0ae45745ed0efa06ff/gst.py#L13-L35)

`selectedOrder` uses the sum of grow–shrink local scores in the finite search, and `selectedDAG` projects the winning order. Lean checks that the order is locally optimal for that same score and the returned arrows are exactly the selected parents. [Lean](../../OpenCausalScience/Papers/AndrewsEtAl2023/OrderSearch.lean)

## Population projection and Proposition 2

Under a locally consistent score for a compositional graphoid, grow–shrink returns the unique Markov boundary in each order's predecessor set. The projected DAG equals the (RU) order DAG and its decomposable score equals the score used in order search. The selected DAG is Markov and edge-deletion minimal. Faithfulness to a DAG supplies the graphoid and composition premises. [Population projection](../../OpenCausalScience/Papers/AndrewsEtAl2023/PopulationProjection.lean)

A one-arrow deletion from a representative preserves containment exactly when the child is independent of the deleted parent given its remaining parents. Score equivalence transports this comparison across representatives. A strictly improving backward search terminates because each step removes an arrow. Chickering’s formalized inclusion transformation supplies an improving deletion whenever the current class contains the truth and is distinct from the true MEC. This yields MEC recovery for a reference that runs BOSS followed by backward equivalence search over all valid deletion neighbors. [Deletion score](../../OpenCausalScience/Papers/AndrewsEtAl2023/DeletionScore.lean) · [Backward search](../../OpenCausalScience/Papers/Chickering2002/BackwardSearch.lean) · [BOSS with BES](../../OpenCausalScience/Papers/AndrewsEtAl2023/WithBackwardSearch.lean) · [Inclusion transformation](chickering-2002-greedy-equivalence-search.md#the-inclusion-transformation)

Proposition 2 assumes i.i.d. sampling from a curved exponential family, causal Markovness, faithfulness and backward equivalence search. The paper uses BIC to supply the local score behavior. The Lean statistical conclusion holds when the local score error for each fixed comparison tends to zero and the sample scores are score-equivalent. A finite union bound transfers the population conclusion to the whole run. The penalized-likelihood proof supplies the local signs from tight null likelihood gains, a positive linear likelihood gap under the alternative, and a diverging sublinear penalty on the extra dimensions. A local score given by differences of a joint-set function is score-equivalent by telescoping over covered reversals. The statistical proof uses finite sampling laws and rational scores. Establishing the likelihood bounds and connecting the paper’s real-valued BIC models to the formal score and equivalence conditions remain open here.
