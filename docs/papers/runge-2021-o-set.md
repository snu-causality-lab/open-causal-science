[OSet.lean](../../OpenCausalScience/Papers/Runge2021/OSet.lean) defines the parents of the outcome and mediators outside the forbidden set, the valid bidirected collider chains, and the parents of their collider nodes for a finite ADMG with a singleton treatment and outcome. `Eligible` takes the separation test relative to the fixed valid ancestor set. `collider_eligible` proves that every vertex included from a chain satisfies both conditions of Definition 3. `o_set_iff_parent_part_without_bidirected` proves the reduction to the parent part when the graph has no bidirected edges. The supplied conditioning set is fixed throughout.

## Forbidden-set avoidance

[OSetForbidden.lean](../../OpenCausalScience/Papers/Runge2021/OSetForbidden.lean) proves `o_set_avoids_forbidden`, the first conjunct of the validity argument in Appendix B.7 for the CGA domain. The directed edges admit a topological ranking and the fixed conditioning vertices are nondescendants of treatment. This last premise is stronger than the source's condition that the fixed set avoid the forbidden set. The proof handles parents of collider nodes, including exclusion of the treatment itself. The same module proves the collider-parent eligibility conclusion of Lemma B.3 on this domain. If a collider parent outside the valid ancestor set is connected to treatment given that set, adjoining its arrow into the collider gives an active walk from the collider to treatment. Reducing the walk to a simple path contradicts the collider's eligibility. The theorem `o_set_eligible` combines that result with the outcome-parent and collider cases.

The module also proves an arrowhead-entry argument used in Appendix B.4 and B.5. When the conditioning set avoids descendants of the outcome and mediators, an active continuation after entering one of these vertices at an arrowhead consists of directed steps and ends at another such descendant. In particular, it cannot reach an endpoint outside the descendant set. The remaining noncausal motifs and the generality of Theorem 1 still need proofs. ## The comparison of adjustment information

[AdjustmentInformation.lean](../../OpenCausalScience/Papers/Runge2021/AdjustmentInformation.lean) proves `adjustment_information_difference`, Equation (S5) in Appendix B.4. It uses the conditional mutual information chain rule as a premise. `comparison_of_two_inequalities` proves Lemma 1's pairwise sufficient direction. Its necessity argument additionally needs a construction of compatible distributions. Theorem 3 needs graph arguments tying the pairwise inequalities to Conditions I and II.

The [CGA comparison](https://github.com/snu-causality-lab/cga/blob/v0.2.0a1/docs/reference/RUNGE_ADJUSTMENT.md#a-defective-sufficiency-step-and-two-qualified-observations) gives a six-vertex graph for the outcome-side step in Appendix B.10, p. 19. [ConditionIExample.lean](../../OpenCausalScience/Papers/Runge2021/ConditionIExample.lean) proves that `Y ← V → U₂` is active given `X` and that adding `V` blocks every path from `Y` to `{U,U₂}`. The O-set and the qualifying Conditions I and II on this example come from the CGA regression. Under the Markov premise, the latter separation supplies the conditional-information zero used by `outcome_comparison_of_separating_subset`. This proves an outcome-side inequality for the recorded comparison. A general argument for finding a separating subset, or another argument establishing the inequality for every comparison, remains open. The optimality conclusion of Theorem 3 is compatible with this example.
